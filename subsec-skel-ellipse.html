    Subsection 3.7.2 Ellipse

    Definition 3.7.6.

    An ellipse is the set of points in a plane the sum of whose distances from two fixed points \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) (the foci) is constant.
    🔗
    🔗

    The Standard Ellipse.

    An ellipse centred at the origin with its foci on the \(x\)-axis has the standard form
    \begin{equation} \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1, \qquad a \gt b.\tag{3.7.3} \end{equation}
    The segment joining \((a,0)\) and \((-a,0)\) is the semi-major axis, the segment joining \((0,b)\) and \((0,-b)\) is the semi-minor axis, and the foci satisfy \(c^2 = a^2 - b^2 \gt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Where do the formulas for \(a\) come from? Two evaluations of Definition 3.7.6 do all the work. Carry them out.
    🔗

    Focal Relation for the Ellipse.

    \begin{equation} a^2 = b^2 + c^2 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad c^2 = a^2 - b^2.\tag{3.7.4} \end{equation}

    🔗
