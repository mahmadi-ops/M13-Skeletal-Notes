Example 2.3.18. A definite integral with no elementary antiderivative.

The function \(e^{-x^2}\) has no elementary antiderivative, so the integral
\begin{equation*} I = \int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx \end{equation*}
cannot be evaluated with the fundamental theorem of calculus. Use the Taylor polynomial of order \(n\) of \(e^{u}\) to approximate \(I\text{,}\) bound the error with the Remainder Estimation Theorem, and find a value of \(n\) that guarantees an error of at most \(10^{-4}\text{.}\)
