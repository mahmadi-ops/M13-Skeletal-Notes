Example 4.5.6. Example I.

Consider the function \(f(x,y) = 1 + x^2 + y^2\text{,}\) the point \(P_0(1,1)\text{,}\) and the unit vector \(\hat{u} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\jmath}\text{.}\) Use the definition of directional derivative to compute \(\left(D_{\hat{u}} f\right)_{P_0}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
🔗
The computation has a useful geometric picture. Along the ray through \(P_0(1,1)\) in the direction \(\hat{u}\text{,}\) the values of \(f\) are a single-variable function \(g(s)\text{.}\) Write it down, and connect it to what you just computed. (Substitute the same two expressions into \(f\text{,}\) but keep \(s\) as a variable rather than letting it go to zero. Then say which derivative of \(g\) your answer above is, and check it by differentiating \(g\) directly.)
