Example 2.3.26. Approximating \(\pi\) using the Taylor series for \(\arctan(x)\).

Use the Taylor series for \(\arctan(x)\) to approximate the value of \(\pi\text{.}\) Use Taylor’s Remainder Theorem to find an upper bound for the error in this approximation, and show that the error decreases as the order \(n\) of the Taylor polynomial increases.
🔗
Solution.
🔗
For the record: the derivatives of \(f(x) = \arctan(x)\) satisfy
\begin{equation} \left|f^{(n+1)}(t)\right| \le n! \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R},\tag{2.3.18} \end{equation}
and the resulting error bound is
\begin{equation} \left|\pi - 4\,T_n(1)\right| \le \frac{4}{n+1}.\tag{2.3.19} \end{equation}
The bound goes to zero as \(n \to \infty\text{,}\) but very slowly: to guarantee an error of at most \(0.01\) one needs \(n \ge 399\text{.}\)
